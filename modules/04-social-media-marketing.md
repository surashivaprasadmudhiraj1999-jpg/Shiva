## Lesson Breakdown

### Lesson 1: Platform Strategy & Audience Architecture

**Concept Explanation (~500 words)**

The most common mistake in social media marketing is treating all platforms identically. Each platform has a distinct **culture, algorithm, content format, and audience expectation**. Advanced practitioners develop platform-specific strategies rooted in where their audience spends time and what content behaviors each platform rewards.

**Platform Profiles (as of mid-2026 — verify current audience demographics)**

*LinkedIn:*
- Audience: Professionals, B2B decision-makers, career-focused individuals.
- Algorithm rewards: Content that generates comments and professional discussion (not just likes). Native documents, personal stories with professional insight, and data-driven posts consistently outperform links.
- Content cadence: 3–5 posts/week for individuals; 1–2 posts/week for brand pages (organic page reach is limited; employees' personal profiles amplify further).
- Best for: B2B lead generation, thought leadership, recruiting, partnership building.

*Instagram:*
- Audience: Consumer brands, lifestyle, fashion, food, travel, B2C; increasingly 25–45 demographic.
- Algorithm rewards: Reels (short-form video) get the widest reach to non-followers; Stories maintain deep engagement with existing followers; static carousel posts drive saves.
- Content cadence: 4–7 posts/week across feed + Stories; 3–5 Reels/week for growth.
- Best for: E-commerce, visual brands, influencer-driven categories, brand awareness.

*TikTok:*
- Audience: Broadest demographic range now (Gen Z through Millennial to Gen X); high consumption, entertainment-first.
- Algorithm: Interest graph (not social graph) — content can reach millions with zero followers. For You Page (FYP) distribution is the dominant reach mechanism.
- Content: Raw, authentic, fast-paced, trend-driven. Highly produced content often underperforms genuine, native-feeling video.
- Best for: Broad consumer awareness, product discovery, brand-building for younger audiences. [FAST-CHANGING: regulatory and ownership status may change]

*YouTube:*
- Audience: All demographics; very strong for how-to, education, review, and entertainment.
- Algorithm: Search-based discovery (like Google) + suggestion engine (related videos). Watch time and click-through rate from thumbnails are the key ranking signals.
- Content: Long-form (8–20 min) for depth and ad revenue; Shorts for reach and discoverability.
- Best for: Long-term SEO-adjacent content, authority building, monetization, education-based marketing.

*X (Twitter):*
- Audience: Tech, finance, media, journalism, politics, crypto communities. [FAST-CHANGING: platform trajectory evolves rapidly]
- Content: Real-time commentary, threads, industry discourse, news.
- Best for: Thought leadership in specific niches, real-time brand presence, customer service.

**The Platform Selection Framework**
Don't try to be everywhere. Select platforms based on:
1. Where does your audience already spend time? (Use SparkToro or audience research to verify)
2. Where does the content format align with your production capacity?
3. Where does your business model fit the platform's conversion behavior?

For most businesses: choose 2 primary platforms to invest in deeply; maintain a presence on 1–2 secondary platforms with lower effort.

**Real-World Case Study**

Notion's social media growth is a widely referenced B2B example. Rather than spreading across all platforms, they focused on building authentic community on Twitter/X (among productivity and maker communities) and YouTube (with long-form tutorials). User-generated content — community members sharing their Notion setups — amplified reach without paid investment. Platform selection was driven by where their target users (knowledge workers, developers) already spent time. (Verify current Notion social strategy as it evolves.)

**AI Tools for This Lesson**

- **SparkToro** — Audience research; shows what websites, social accounts, and podcasts your target audience actually follows. [Verify pricing]
- **Claude / ChatGPT** — Build platform strategy documents and editorial frameworks.
  - *Prompt:* "Build a social media platform strategy for a [business type] with a team of [X people] and a content budget of [$Y/month]. Recommend 2 primary platforms with rationale, content pillars, posting cadence, and primary KPIs. Format as a strategic brief."
- **Hootsuite / Buffer / Sprout Social** — Cross-platform scheduling and analytics.

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**

- Being present on every platform at low quality — depth on two platforms beats shallow presence on six.
- Using the same content across all platforms without adaptation — a LinkedIn thought leadership post repurposed verbatim as an Instagram caption fails both audiences.
- Targeting platforms where your audience *should* be rather than where they *actually* are — verify with data, not assumption.

### Lesson 2: AI-Powered Content Production Systems

**Concept Explanation (~500 words)**

Consistency is the single biggest predictor of social media growth, and consistency requires volume — more content than most teams can sustainably produce manually. AI changes this equation by handling the production layer while humans focus on strategy, editing, and authenticity injection.

**The Content Pillar System**
Before using AI, define 3–5 **content pillars** — the recurring themes your account consistently addresses. Every piece of content maps to a pillar.

Example pillars for a marketing agency:
1. Marketing strategy and insights (thought leadership)
2. Client results and case studies (social proof)
3. Tool reviews and tactical how-tos (education)
4. Behind-the-scenes and team culture (humanization)
5. Industry news commentary (relevance and timeliness)

With defined pillars, AI-assisted content production becomes directing the AI to produce within each pillar — not trying to generate content ideas from scratch.

**The AI Content Production Workflow**

*Step 1 — Ideation:*
Prompt AI to generate a month's worth of content ideas within each pillar. Evaluate, curate, and mark ideas as approved or discarded.

*Step 2 — Drafting:*
For each approved idea, prompt AI to draft the full post: caption, hook, body, CTA, and suggested hashtags (where relevant).

*Step 3 — Brand Voice Editing:*
Humans review every draft for: brand voice consistency, factual accuracy, specificity (adding real data, real stories), and removal of generic AI tells.

*Step 4 — Visual Production:*
Generate or select visuals. AI image generators (Midjourney, DALL-E, Adobe Firefly) can produce brand-consistent social visuals at scale. Video: tools like CapCut, Descript, or Runway can assist with editing and captions. [FAST-CHANGING: AI video tools evolving extremely rapidly]

*Step 5 — Scheduling:*
Schedule via Buffer, Hootsuite, or Later. Some tools include AI-suggested optimal posting times based on audience activity data.

*Step 6 — Performance review loop:*
Weekly: review last week's content performance. Identify the top 3 posts. Ask: why did these work? Incorporate the winning patterns into the next week's AI prompts.

**Repurposing Content at Scale**
One long-form asset (YouTube video, blog post, webinar) can become:
- 5 LinkedIn posts (one insight per post)
- 3 TikTok/Reels scripts (short, platform-native)
- 10 Twitter/X threads or standalone tweets
- 1 email newsletter
- 3 Instagram carousels
- 1 podcast episode outline

AI handles the repurposing transformation; you supply the original insight source.

**Real-World Case Study**

Gary Vaynerchuk's media team pioneered what they call the "Pillar Content" model — one long-form piece of content per week (keynote speech, YouTube interview) that is then atomized by a team into 30–64 micro-pieces of content across platforms. While GaryVee's team is human-driven, the workflow is now achievable by small teams using AI. The principle: create from depth, distribute at breadth.

**AI Tools for This Lesson** [FAST-CHANGING — verify current capabilities]

- **Claude / ChatGPT** — Ideation, drafting, repurposing, voice adaptation.
- **Jasper** — Marketing-focused AI writing with social media templates.
- **Descript** — AI video editing: transcription, filler-word removal, audio enhancement.
- **CapCut** — Video editing with AI caption generation, widely used for TikTok/Reels.
- **Midjourney / Adobe Firefly / DALL-E** — AI image generation for social visuals.
- **Canva Magic Studio** — AI design assistance within the Canva workflow.
- **Buffer / Later** — AI caption suggestions + scheduling.

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**

- Publishing unedited AI copy — AI social posts are detectable to savvy audiences and often feel hollow. Human editing is non-negotiable.
- Not defining content pillars before prompting AI — without pillars, AI produces generic content that doesn't build brand identity.
- Treating repurposing as copy-paste — adapt for each platform's format and audience expectation.
- Overproducing and under-engaging — 20 posts a week with no community engagement outperforms 0 community engagement but underperforms 10 posts + active replies.

### Lesson 3: Algorithm Mastery & Organic Reach Optimization

**Concept Explanation (~500 words)**

Platform algorithms determine who sees your content. Understanding how each algorithm works — and creating content that the algorithm distributes — is the leverage point for organic growth without paid spend.

**Universal Algorithm Principles**
Despite platform-specific differences, most social algorithms share common logic:

1. **Engagement velocity matters.** Content that generates strong engagement (comments, shares, saves) in the first 30–60 minutes is shown to a wider audience. This is why posting at the right time for your specific audience matters.

2. **Completion rate / watch time.** Video content is scored on how much of it people watch. Hook failures (people swipe or scroll away in the first 2–3 seconds) suppress distribution. This makes the opening frame or line critical.

3. **Saves and shares outweigh likes.** A save signals "I want to come back to this" — high utility. A share extends reach. Likes are the weakest engagement signal on most platforms.

4. **Content type preferences.** Platforms promote the formats they want creators to use. When a platform launches a new format (Reels on Instagram, LinkedIn Documents, YouTube Shorts), they temporarily over-distribute that content type to encourage adoption. Early adopters benefit disproportionately.

5. **Account-level trust.** Consistent posting, low violation history, and positive engagement patterns build account-level trust that gives your content a higher initial distribution probability.

**Platform-Specific Algorithm Notes**

*LinkedIn:* The first 60–90 minutes after posting are critical. Reply to every comment immediately during this window — the algorithm measures conversation richness. Native documents (PDF carousels) get significantly higher reach than external links in posts.

*Instagram Reels:* Watch time and shares to DMs/Stories are the primary distribution signals. A strong hook (first frame visual + text overlay) determines whether the algorithm distributes past the initial test audience.

*TikTok:* The FYP algorithm is interest-graph-driven, not follower-driven. Each video is independently evaluated and can reach any audience regardless of account size. The reward signal is completion rate and re-watches. Loops (videos that naturally loop) game this metric.

*YouTube:* Click-through rate from thumbnail + title (what gets the impression to become a click) and watch time / average percentage viewed are the primary signals. Thumbnails and titles deserve as much attention as the video itself.

**Content Timing**
Use platform analytics to identify when YOUR audience is most active (not generic "best time to post" guides — your audience is specific). Schedule posts 5–10 minutes before peak activity windows.

**AI Tools for This Lesson**

- **Sprout Social / Hootsuite Analytics** — Audience activity windows, post performance analysis.
- **VidIQ / TubeBuddy** — YouTube-specific analytics and optimization. [Verify current features]
- **Claude / ChatGPT** — Analyze your top-performing posts and identify winning patterns.
  - *Prompt:* "Analyze these 10 social media posts and their engagement data. Identify: (1) the 3 most common characteristics of high-performing posts, (2) the 3 most common characteristics of underperforming posts, (3) 5 actionable recommendations for improving future content. Posts and data: [PASTE]"

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**

- Posting links in LinkedIn post bodies — LinkedIn suppresses external link posts. Put links in the first comment.
- Using generic "best time to post" research — every audience is different; use your own analytics.
- Abandoning content that doesn't perform immediately — some platforms (especially YouTube and LinkedIn) distribute content for weeks after posting; a "slow burn" piece can accumulate reach over time.
- Buying followers or engagement — suppresses organic reach as the algorithm detects low engagement rates relative to follower count.

### Lesson 4: Community Building & Engagement Systems

**Concept Explanation (~450 words)**

Follower count is a vanity metric. Community engagement is the signal that drives algorithm distribution, builds brand loyalty, and converts audience attention into business outcomes. Advanced social media practitioners build **engagement systems** — structured processes for consistent, meaningful community interaction.

**The Engagement Flywheel**
Engagement drives algorithm reach → algorithm reach brings new followers → new followers become community members → community engagement drives algorithm reach.

To activate the flywheel:
1. **Reply to every comment** within 2–4 hours of posting (within 60 minutes if possible). Responses invite further replies, building thread depth that the algorithm rewards.
2. **Ask questions in your posts** to invite comments — not generic questions, but specific, debatable, or experience-based prompts that your audience genuinely has opinions on.
3. **Comment on other accounts** in your niche — particularly accounts your target audience follows. Thoughtful comments on high-traffic posts put your name in front of new audiences.
4. **Feature community members** — reshare user-generated content, quote community members' insights, create "community spotlight" content. This incentivizes others to engage knowing they might be featured.
5. **Build in DM conversations** — for B2B/service businesses especially, the most valuable relationships are built 1:1 in DMs, not in comment sections.

**From Community to Revenue**
Social engagement is mid-funnel activity. Converting followers to customers requires clear pathways:
- **Link in bio** (Instagram/TikTok) to a landing page, product, or lead magnet.
- **Direct-response CTAs** in post captions: "Comment [WORD] and I'll DM you the guide" (triggers automated DM delivery via ManyChat or similar). [FAST-CHANGING: platform rules on automated DMs vary and change]
- **Stories polls and questions** that identify intent signals, followed by DM outreach to high-intent responders.
- **Live sessions** (LinkedIn Live, Instagram Live, YouTube Live) — the highest-conversion social format for services and high-ticket products.

**Handling Negative Comments and Crises**
Advanced practitioners have a response protocol:
- Acknowledge negative feedback publicly, resolve in DMs.
- Never delete critical comments unless they violate community guidelines (transparency builds trust).
- For genuine crises, respond quickly with facts; do not speculate.
- Have pre-approved crisis communication templates ready to adapt.

**AI Tools for This Lesson**

- **ManyChat** — Instagram/Facebook/WhatsApp DM automation for comment-triggered flows. [FAST-CHANGING: verify platform terms compliance]
- **Sprout Social / Hootsuite** — Unified inbox for managing comments and DMs across platforms.
- **Claude / ChatGPT** — Draft response templates for common question types, positive feedback, and complaint handling.

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**

- Posting without engaging back — a post without responses to comments signals to the algorithm (and your audience) that you're broadcasting, not conversing.
- Automated generic responses — bots responding to comments with "Thanks for engaging!" are immediately recognizable and damage trust.
- Ignoring DMs — for service businesses, DMs are often where sales happen.

### Lesson 5: Influencer & Creator Marketing

**Concept Explanation (~500 words)**

Influencer marketing has matured from experimental to essential for many brands. Advanced practitioners treat it as a channel with rigorous systems: identification criteria, vetting processes, clear briefs, and attribution frameworks — not a collection of one-off sponsorships.

**Creator Tier Definitions** (Approximate — follower counts vary by platform and niche)

| Tier | Followers | Characteristics |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| Mega | 1M+ | Mass reach; lower engagement rate; high cost; brand safety risk |
| Macro | 100K–1M | Broad reach; professional content quality |
| Mid-tier | 50K–100K | Strong engagement; often niche-specific |
| Micro | 10K–50K | Highest engagement rates; strong community trust; niche authority |
| Nano | 1K–10K | Hyper-niche; exceptional trust; low cost; scalable in volume |

For most brands, **micro and mid-tier** creators deliver the best ROI: authentic engagement + affordable rates + niche-specific credibility.

**The Vetting Framework**
Follower count is the least important vetting criterion. Evaluate:
1. **Engagement rate** — Likes + comments / followers. Industry benchmarks vary; compare within creator's niche and platform.
2. **Engagement authenticity** — Scan comments for genuine conversation vs. bot-like "Great post!" clusters. Tools like HypeAuditor or Modash detect fake followers and engagement.
3. **Audience alignment** — Does their audience match your ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)? Request audience demographics from the creator or use a platform tool.
4. **Content quality and consistency** — Does their content quality match your brand? Are they a consistent creator or posting irregularly?
5. **Brand safety** — Review historical posts for content that conflicts with your brand values.
6. **Previous brand partnerships** — Have they worked with direct competitors? Do they disclose partnerships properly (#ad, #sponsored)?

**The Creator Brief**
A strong brief gives creators enough guidance to stay on-brand while leaving room for their authentic voice (which is what their audience trusts). Over-scripting kills authenticity.

Brief elements:
- Campaign objective (awareness / conversion / content generation)
- Key message — the ONE thing you want the audience to take away
- Required mentions — specific features, offers, or product names to include
- Required disclosures — #ad, #sponsored, per FTC/ASA guidelines
- Content format and platform specifications
- Timeline: draft submission, feedback window, posting date
- Usage rights — can the brand repurpose the content? For how long? In paid ads?

**Contracting Essentials**
Always contract. Key clauses:
- Deliverables (specific posts, stories, videos)
- Timelines and deadlines
- Approval process
- Payment terms
- Exclusivity period (can they work with competitors during/after?)
- Content usage rights — especially important if repurposing in paid ads
- FTC/ASA disclosure requirements

**Measuring Influencer ROI**

- **Awareness campaigns:** Reach, impressions, Share of Voice change, branded search volume lift (Google Trends).
- **Conversion campaigns:** Use unique discount codes or UTM-tagged links per creator to attribute clicks and conversions.
- **UGC collection:** Asset value — how much would it cost to produce equivalent content through a production studio?
- **Incrementality:** Use a holdout test (pause creator campaigns for a period) to measure actual incremental revenue contribution.

**Real-World Case Study**

Gymshark's early growth was driven almost entirely by micro and mid-tier fitness influencers who received free product and commission-based affiliate links rather than flat-fee payments. By scaling across hundreds of fitness content creators rather than investing in a few mega-influencers, they built authentic reach within their exact target community at a fraction of the cost. They later scaled to macro and celebrity partnerships as revenue grew — but the micro-influencer foundation built the brand's credibility. (Verify current Gymshark influencer strategy as it evolves.)

**AI Tools for This Lesson**

- **HypeAuditor / Modash / Grin** — Creator discovery, vetting, and campaign management. [FAST-CHANGING]
- **Aspire / CreatorIQ** — Enterprise influencer marketing platforms.
- **Claude / ChatGPT** — Draft creator briefs, contracts templates, and outreach emails.
  - *Prompt:* "Write a creator brief for a sponsored Instagram Reel + Story campaign for [PRODUCT]. Campaign goal: [AWARENESS/CONVERSION]. Required: mention [KEY FEATURE] and include the discount code [CODE]. Brand voice: [TONE]. Budget: [$X]. Content must be approved before posting. Include: background, campaign objective, messaging guidance, dos and don'ts, usage rights note, and posting timeline."

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**

- Choosing creators by follower count without vetting engagement authenticity.
- Not contracting — handshake deals leave both parties exposed.
- Over-scripting the brief — creators know their audience best; let them tell your story in their voice.
- Forgetting usage rights — repurposing creator content in paid ads without a usage rights clause is a legal risk.
- Not disclosing paid partnerships — FTC and ASA regulations require clear disclosure; non-compliance risks fines and reputational damage.

### Lesson 6: Social Media Analytics & Attribution

**Concept Explanation (~450 words)**

Social media analytics should drive decisions, not just report activity. Advanced practitioners build measurement systems that connect social content to business outcomes, not just reach and engagement metrics.

**Tier 1: Platform Native Analytics**
Every platform provides native analytics — Instagram Insights, LinkedIn Analytics, TikTok Analytics, YouTube Studio. These show:
- Reach and impressions (exposure)
- Engagement (likes, comments, shares, saves)
- Follower growth
- Video performance (watch time, completion rate)
- Link clicks (where applicable)
- Audience demographics

Useful for operational decisions (what content to make more of), but limited for business attribution.

**Tier 2: Third-Party Social Analytics**
Tools like Sprout Social, Hootsuite Analytics, and Brandwatch aggregate data across platforms, add competitive benchmarking, and provide deeper historical analysis. Useful for reporting, competitive intelligence, and team efficiency.

**Tier 3: Business Attribution**
The hardest part of social media measurement. How do you connect a LinkedIn post to a closed deal 6 weeks later?

Approaches:
- **UTM parameters on all link posts** — Track social traffic in GA4, see landing page conversion rates by platform and campaign.
- **Social-sourced CRM opportunities** — Train sales teams to ask and record "how did you find us?" in CRM. Organic social often surfaces in responses even when it's not the last click.
- **Brand search volume tracking** — Social brand awareness lifts branded search. Track branded keyword search volume in Google Search Console and Google Trends alongside social activity. A correlation is not causation but is a directional signal.
- **Social commerce tracking** — Instagram Shopping, TikTok Shop, Pinterest Shopping provide direct purchase attribution. [FAST-CHANGING: social commerce features vary by platform and region]

**Key Social Media KPIs by Business Goal**

| Business Goal | Primary KPIs |
|--------------|-------------|
| Brand awareness | Reach, impressions, branded search volume |
| Lead generation | Link clicks, landing page conversion rate, leads sourced from social |
| Community building | Engagement rate, follower growth rate, comment quality |
| Revenue | Revenue attributed (UTM), social-assisted conversions in GA4 |
| Retention | DM volume, community size, repeat engagement rate |

**Share of Voice**
A strategic metric: what percentage of total online conversation in your category mentions your brand vs. competitors? Track with Brandwatch, Mention, or Sprout Social. Growing Share of Voice in organic social signals increasing brand salience.

**AI Tools for This Lesson**

- **Sprout Social / Hootsuite** — Cross-platform analytics and reporting.
- **Brandwatch / Mention** — Social listening and Share of Voice tracking.
- **Google Analytics 4** — Business attribution via UTM-tagged social traffic.
- **Claude / ChatGPT** — Analyze performance data and generate insights narratives.

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**

- Reporting impressions to executive stakeholders — they want pipeline and revenue.
- Not using UTM parameters on social links — losing attribution data in GA4.
- Measuring engagement rate without context — 1% engagement on LinkedIn is strong; 1% on TikTok is below average. Know platform benchmarks.

### Lesson 7: Building a Sustainable Social Media Operation

**Concept Explanation (~400 words)**

Most social media programs fail not because of strategy but because of unsustainable operations. Publishing consistently across multiple platforms while engaging genuinely and tracking performance requires systems, workflows, and realistic resource planning.

**The Social Media Operations Stack**

*Planning layer:* Monthly content calendar with pillar distribution, campaign integrations, and seasonal moments. Built 4–6 weeks ahead. Updated weekly.

*Production layer:* Content batching — produce a week's worth of content in one focused session rather than creating daily. AI handles first drafts; human editing and approval follows.

*Scheduling layer:* All approved content is scheduled via a social media management tool. Reduces daily manual effort; ensures consistent posting even during busy periods.

*Engagement layer:* Daily 20–30 minute engagement windows — responding to comments, DMing new followers, commenting on relevant posts. This is not automatable; it requires human judgment.

*Analysis layer:* Weekly 30-minute performance review; monthly deeper analysis and strategy adjustments.

**Repurposing Content: The Force Multiplier**
Every piece of original content should be repurposed 3–5 times before moving on. A single YouTube video becomes: a LinkedIn native document, 3 Reels/TikTok scripts, a newsletter feature, and an email. This multiplies output without proportionally multiplying effort.

**Team Structure Options**

| Team Size | Recommended Approach |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Solo / Freelancer | 2 platforms max, AI-assisted production, batch creation weekly |
| 2–3 person team | 3 platforms, dedicated platform owners, shared content calendar |
| Agency / 5+ | Full specialization by platform, dedicated community manager, paid social integration |

**Preventing Burnout**
Social media marketing, especially community management, is emotionally demanding and algorithmically relentless. Build sustainability in:
- Content batching prevents daily creative exhaustion
- Clear posting frequency commitments (3x/week is better sustained than daily)
- "Social media off" hours in team schedules
- Rotating coverage for community management

**AI Tools for This Lesson**

- **Notion / Airtable** — Content calendar and workflow management.
- **Buffer / Later / Publer** — Social scheduling and basic analytics.
- **Claude / ChatGPT** — Content batch-drafting sessions.
- **Loom** — Async video review of content drafts for remote teams.

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**

- No editorial calendar — reactive content production leads to gaps and low quality.
- Posting frequency that isn't sustainable — better to commit to 3x/week and maintain it for 6 months than post daily for 3 weeks and burn out.
- No approval workflow for teams/agencies — brand-inconsistent posts happen without clear review gates.

### Task 2: Content Production Sprint

**Objective:** Using AI tools, produce a full month of social media content for one platform — including captions, visuals (or visual briefs), and a scheduling plan.

**Deliverable:** 20 finished posts (caption + visual or visual brief) + a scheduling plan + a performance hypothesis for each post.

**Step-by-Step Workflow**

*AI-Assisted Version:*
1. Choose one platform (LinkedIn, Instagram, or TikTok) and a brand (your own or a provided brief).

4. For each post, use Canva or an AI image generator to create the visual (or write a specific visual brief for a designer).
5. Write a scheduling plan: which posts go on which dates, at what times, and why.
6. For each post, write a one-sentence "performance hypothesis": "This post will outperform average because ___."
7. Compile everything into a shareable content bank (Notion, Google Drive folder, or Airtable).

Write all captions without AI assistance. Produce or brief visuals manually.

| Criterion | Points |

### Task 3: Influencer Campaign Design & Execution

**Objective:** Design and partially execute an influencer marketing campaign — from creator identification and vetting through brief creation and outreach.

**Deliverable:** Creator shortlist with vetting analysis + campaign brief + 10 outreach emails sent (with tracking).

**Step-by-Step Workflow**

*AI-Assisted Version:*
1. Define the campaign: objective, product/service, target audience, platform, budget ($0–$5,000 — can use gifting/free product if no cash budget).

6. Use Claude to draft 10 personalized outreach emails — one per shortlisted creator, referencing a specific piece of their content.
7. Send outreach and track responses in the Google Sheet: Date Sent | Response? | Agreed? | Negotiated Rate | Status.

*Manual Version:*
Write all outreach emails and the brief manually.

| Criterion | Points |
|-----------|--------|

## Assessment

### Multiple-Choice Quiz (10 Questions)

**Q1.** On LinkedIn, which content format typically receives the HIGHEST organic reach?

- A) External link posts directing to a blog article
- B) Native document uploads (PDF carousels)
- C) Reposted content from other LinkedIn users
- D) Company page job postings

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: LinkedIn's algorithm suppresses external link posts to reduce users leaving the platform. Native content formats — especially documents (PDF carousels) and native video — receive significantly higher organic distribution.*

---

**Q2.** TikTok's content distribution algorithm is PRIMARILY based on which system?

- A) Social graph — content shown to followers of the account
- B) Interest graph — content shown based on topic interest regardless of follower relationship
- C) Chronological feed — newest content shown first
- D) Paid promotion — only boosted content reaches non-followers

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: TikTok's For You Page (FYP) distributes content based on interest signals, not follower relationships. This is why accounts with zero followers can reach millions — each video is independently evaluated and shown to users whose interest patterns suggest they'd engage.*

---

**Q3.** An Instagram Reel receives 50,000 views but only 20 comments. What is the MOST likely explanation?

- A) The video had poor audio quality
- B) The content was watched but didn't create a strong emotional response or call to action that invited comments
- C) Instagram is suppressing the post due to a guideline violation
- D) The creator has too many followers for the algorithm to distribute the post widely

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: High views with low comments typically means the content was entertaining or informative enough to watch (low-friction) but didn't create a strong enough emotional pull or explicit invitation to comment. Adding a question CTA or a debatable opinion in the caption typically increases comment rates.*

---

**Q4.** Which influencer tier typically delivers the HIGHEST engagement rate relative to follower count?

- A) Mega influencers (1M+ followers)
- B) Macro influencers (100K–1M followers)
- C) Micro influencers (10K–50K followers)
- D) The tier with the most followers always has the highest engagement rate

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation: Micro influencers typically have the highest engagement rates proportionally because their audiences are more niche, personally connected to the creator, and their communities are built on genuine interest rather than mass celebrity appeal.*

---

**Q5.** A brand wants to measure whether its social media activity is driving website conversions. Which setup is ESSENTIAL?

- A) Posting a link in every social media bio
- B) UTM parameters on all links shared in social posts, tracked in GA4
- C) Running a social media poll to ask followers if they've visited the website
- D) Checking platform native analytics for "profile visits"

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: UTM parameters (source, medium, campaign) tagged on all social links allow GA4 to correctly attribute website sessions, conversions, and revenue to specific social platforms and campaigns. Without UTMs, social traffic often gets misattributed to "direct" or "unknown" in analytics.*

---

**Q6.** According to platform algorithm principles, which engagement signal is MOST valuable for content distribution on most platforms?

- A) Likes (reactions)
- B) Follower count of the account
- C) Shares and saves
- D) Profile views generated by the post

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation: Saves (bookmarks) signal high utility — the viewer wants to return to the content later. Shares extend organic reach by distributing content to new audiences. Both are weighted more heavily than passive likes in most platform algorithms because they represent stronger positive intent.*

---

**Q7.** A creator partnership agreement does NOT include usage rights language. The brand repurposes the creator's video in a paid Meta ad. What is the MOST likely outcome?

- A) Nothing; brands can freely use content they paid creators to make
- B) The creator may legally demand the content be removed and seek compensation for unauthorized commercial use
- C) Meta will automatically reject the ad
- D) The creator's account will be suspended for participating in paid advertising

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Without a usage rights clause in the contract, the creator retains intellectual property over their content. Using it in paid advertising — which is a different commercial use case than organic posting — typically requires explicit licensing. Brands that repurpose without rights risk legal action.*

---

**Q8.** A social media manager wants to improve organic reach on Instagram. Which action is MOST likely to have the GREATEST impact?

- A) Increasing posting frequency from 3 to 7 posts per week
- B) Producing more Reels with strong hooks and actively responding to all comments within the first hour
- C) Adding more hashtags to each post
- D) Switching all posts to Stories format

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Reels receive the highest organic distribution potential on Instagram. The first hour of engagement (especially comments and replies, which create thread depth) signals to the algorithm that content is generating conversation — boosting distribution to wider audiences.*

---

**Q9.** What does "Share of Voice" measure in social media analytics?

- A) The number of times a brand's posts are shared by followers
- B) The percentage of total online brand mentions in a category that belong to your brand vs. competitors
- C) The percentage of a platform's total ad impressions purchased by the brand
- D) The number of social platforms on which a brand is active

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Share of Voice (SOV) measures what proportion of total conversation in your market or category involves your brand. A growing SOV typically precedes market share gains and indicates improving brand salience.*

---

**Q10.** A B2B brand is debating between investing in a LinkedIn strategy or a TikTok strategy to reach their target audience (enterprise software buyers). Which framework should guide this decision?

- A) TikTok always has higher reach, so it should be the default choice
- B) Choose LinkedIn because it is a B2B platform by definition
- C) Research where the target audience actually spends time, what content they engage with there, and which platform's conversion behavior fits the sales process
- D) Choose both platforms equally, as omnipresence is more important than focus

**Correct Answer: C**
*Explanation: Platform selection must be audience-led, not assumption-led. While LinkedIn is typically stronger for B2B, the correct decision requires verifying where the ICP actually spends time (SparkToro, audience research), what content they engage with, and how the platform's typical user behavior fits a high-consideration B2B sales cycle.*

### Short-Answer Questions

**Q1.** A client's organic social media reach has dropped significantly over the past 6 months despite maintaining the same posting frequency. What are the three most likely causes and how would you diagnose each?

**Model Answer:**

1. **Algorithm change.** Every major platform updates its distribution algorithm periodically, and tactics that worked 6 months ago may be suppressed today. *Diagnose with:* Review platform announcements (LinkedIn, Instagram, TikTok all publish creator guidance). Check industry publications (Social Media Examiner, Later Blog) for algorithm change reporting around the decline period. Compare the content types being penalized vs. those the platform is currently promoting.

2. **Creative fatigue / format misalignment.** If the same content format and style has been used for months without evolution, the audience may be less responsive and the algorithm detects the reduced engagement, suppressing reach. *Diagnose with:* Pull platform analytics and compare engagement rate and completion rates over time — a declining engagement rate alongside declining reach confirms creative fatigue. Compare the current content mix (% Reels vs. static, % native video vs. links) against the platform's currently rewarded formats.

3. **Increased competition or audience shift.** More accounts creating content in the same niche increases competition for audience attention. The ICP may have migrated to a newer platform. *Diagnose with:* Use Sprout Social or Brandwatch to measure Share of Voice trend — if total category conversation is growing but brand share is shrinking, it's a competitive displacement issue. Check Google Trends and platform audience data for shifts in where the ICP is active.

---

**Q2.** Explain why an influencer's published follower count is often the LEAST useful metric when evaluating partnership potential, and describe the metrics you would prioritize instead.

**Model Answer:**
Follower count is a lagging indicator of a creator's past growth, not current audience quality or engagement. Follower numbers are easily inflated through purchased followers, giveaway tactics, or viral moments with audiences unrelated to the brand's target customer. A creator with 500,000 followers that include 40% purchased bots, a disengaged audience from a viral trend unrelated to your niche, and a 0.3% engagement rate is far less valuable than a creator with 25,000 highly engaged, niche-specific followers and a 4% engagement rate.

Metrics I would prioritize:

1. **Engagement rate** (likes + comments / followers) — Measures what proportion of the audience is actively responding. Average varies by platform and niche; compare within category.
2. **Comment quality** — Genuine conversation, questions, and personal responses vs. emoji-only or copy-paste-looking comments. Manual review of the comment section is the most reliable bot-detection method for smaller budgets.
3. **Audience demographics** — Age, gender, location, and interests of their audience (request from the creator or use a vetting platform). Does it match your ICP?
4. **Past brand partnership performance** — If they've worked with similar brands, can they share performance data? Creators willing to share CPM, click data, or attribution data from past campaigns are more credible.
5. **Content consistency** — Are they posting regularly with consistent quality? Inconsistent creators can't deliver on campaign timelines.

---

**Q3.** A solo consultant wants to build a personal brand on LinkedIn to generate inbound client leads. They have 1–2 hours per week available for social media. What specific, prioritized strategy would you recommend?

**Model Answer:**
With 1–2 hours per week, sustainability and focus are the priorities. A strategy that requires 20 hours/week is a strategy that won't be followed.

**Recommended approach:**

1. **Define one content pillar:** Rather than covering 5 topics, pick one specific problem the ideal client has and become the go-to voice on that topic. Narrowness beats breadth for inbound lead generation.

2. **One post per week, optimized for comments.** Quality over quantity on LinkedIn — one strong post per week that ends with a genuine question or debate prompt outperforms seven average posts. Use AI (Claude, ChatGPT) to draft in 15 minutes; spend 15 minutes editing for authenticity and specificity.

3. **20–30 minutes of engagement daily (or near-daily).** The highest ROI activity on LinkedIn is meaningful comments on posts by other creators, prospect accounts, and content your ideal client engages with. This puts your name in front of the right audience without requiring them to find your own content. Set a 20-minute daily timer.

4. **Optimize the profile as a landing page.** Headline, About section, and Featured section should clearly state: who you help, the specific problem you solve, and a CTA (book a call, download a resource). This is a one-time investment of 2 hours that converts visitors to leads indefinitely.

5. **Measure by inbound DMs and calls booked,** not follower count or likes. Set a 90-day benchmark: at least 2–3 conversations per month initiated from LinkedIn by the end of the period.
