## Lesson Breakdown

### Lesson 1: Topical Authority Architecture

**Concept Explanation (~500 words)**

In 2024–2026, Google's Helpful Content system and the proliferation of AI-generated content fundamentally changed what it means to rank. Individual keyword targeting is dead for advanced practitioners. What wins is **topical authority**: owning an entire subject matter so thoroughly that Google's systems treat your site as the definitive resource.

Topical authority is built through **content clusters** — a hub-and-spoke architecture where a single pillar page covers a broad topic comprehensively, and multiple cluster pages cover sub-topics in depth, all internally linked back to the pillar. This structure signals to Google that your site is the end-to-end expert on a subject.

To build one:

1. **Define your topic universe.** Start with your core offering (e.g., "email marketing software") and map every sub-topic a buyer might research: deliverability, segmentation, A/B testing, GDPR compliance, onboarding sequences. This becomes your content map.

2. **Validate with search demand.** Use Ahrefs, Semrush, or Google Search Console to verify that each sub-topic has real search demand. Prioritize by a combination of search volume, keyword difficulty, and business relevance.

3. **Identify coverage gaps.** Run a competitive gap analysis to find sub-topics your competitors rank for that you don't. These are fast-track opportunities.

4. **Build the pillar first.** A pillar page should be 3,000–5,000 words, cover the topic end-to-end at a strategic level, and link out to every cluster page. Think of it as the table of contents to your cluster.

5. **Sequence cluster production.** Build clusters in order of business impact. Transactional intent (bottom of funnel) earns revenue faster; informational content (top of funnel) builds authority over time.

The advanced move is to use **programmatic SEO** for scalable cluster creation — templated pages built from structured data, appropriate for categories, locations, integrations, or comparisons pages. Handled correctly, this can multiply your indexed pages from hundreds to thousands while maintaining quality.

**Real-World Case Study**

HubSpot's "Topic Cluster" strategy, publicly documented and often cited in the industry, transformed their organic growth. Rather than targeting isolated keywords, they reorganized their entire blog around pillar pages for topics like "email marketing," "inbound marketing," and "CRM." Each pillar linked to dozens of supporting articles. Within months of restructuring, their organic traffic increased significantly. The key insight: Google rewarded the *coherence* of their content network, not just individual pages. (Note: Verify current HubSpot case study figures directly from their published marketing blog, as exact metrics may have been updated.)

**AI Tools for This Lesson**

- **Clearscope / MarketMuse / Surfer SEO** — Content optimization and topical gap analysis. Use these to generate a "content brief" that shows what sub-topics a page must cover to compete.
  - *How to use:* Enter your target keyword → review the "Content Score" suggestions → identify missing entities and questions → integrate into your content outline.
- **ChatGPT / Claude** — Brainstorm topic cluster maps and sub-topic trees.
  - *Prompt:* "You are an SEO strategist. For the topic [X], map out a complete content cluster: one pillar page topic and 15 cluster sub-topics. For each sub-topic, note the likely search intent (informational/commercial/transactional)."
- **Screaming Frog + GPT integration** — Bulk-analyze existing URLs for topic gaps and internal linking opportunities. [FAST-CHANGING: AI integrations in crawlers evolve rapidly]

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**

- Building cluster pages before the pillar exists — Google can't follow the logical hierarchy.
- Making all cluster content the same word count and format — vary depth and format (video, tools, calculators) to signal genuine expertise.
- Ignoring internal linking — clusters without pillar links don't transfer authority.
- Conflating volume with relevance — high-volume keywords that don't connect to your product funnel waste resources.

### Lesson 2: Advanced Keyword Research & Search Intent Mapping

**Concept Explanation (~500 words)**

Keyword research at an advanced level is about **intent architecture**, not keyword lists. A keyword only has value if it aligns with the right stage of the buyer journey and the right type of content you can realistically produce and rank with.

The modern keyword research process:

**Step 1 — Seed and expand.**
Start with 5–10 seed keywords from your core product/service. Use tools like Ahrefs' Keywords Explorer or Semrush's Keyword Magic Tool to expand into thousands of related terms. Export and work in a spreadsheet.

**Step 2 — Cluster by intent.**
Group keywords into four intent buckets:
- **Informational** ("what is content marketing") — blog posts, guides
- **Navigational** ("HubSpot content calendar template") — brand/tool pages
- **Commercial investigation** ("best SEO tools 2026") — comparison/review content
- **Transactional** ("buy Semrush subscription") — landing pages, product pages

**Step 3 — SERP analysis.**
For every keyword you plan to target, manually analyze the top 10 results. What format wins? (List post? Long-form guide? Video? Tool?) What domain authority do the winners have? This tells you the *real* competition level and the content format you must produce.

**Step 4 — Keyword difficulty reality check.**
Domain Rating (DR) and Page Authority are rough proxies. The real metric is whether the page you're competing against has more backlinks, better entity coverage, and stronger brand signals than you can realistically build in 90 days. Be honest — some keywords are not worth targeting now.

**Step 5 — Opportunity scoring.**
Score each keyword cluster on: (search volume × CTR estimate) / (difficulty × resource cost). Prioritize clusters with the highest opportunity score relative to your current authority level.

**Advanced technique — "Keyword cannibalization audit."**
Use Google Search Console + Screaming Frog to find pages on your site competing for the same query. Consolidate or redirect to avoid splitting ranking signals.

**Real-World Case Study**

Zapier grew to millions of monthly organic visits largely through programmatic SEO targeting integration-based keywords: "[App A] + [App B] integration." Each page targeted a transactional keyword with commercial intent. The keyword research insight was simple: people searching for "Slack Trello integration" are actively trying to solve a workflow problem — high intent, low competition at the time. By templating content across thousands of app combinations, they built topical authority in the "app integration" space without writing each page manually. (Verify current Zapier traffic figures via Ahrefs or Semrush, as they change.)

**AI Tools for This Lesson**

- **Ahrefs / Semrush** — Industry-standard keyword research platforms.
- **AlsoAsked / AnswerThePublic** — Map question-based long-tail keywords.
- **Claude / ChatGPT** — Classify and cluster keyword lists at scale.
  - *Prompt:* "Classify the following 50 keywords by search intent (informational, commercial, navigational, transactional) and group them into content clusters. Return a table with columns: Keyword | Intent | Cluster Name. Keywords: [paste list]"
- **Google Search Console** — Free; reveals actual queries driving traffic to your site, including hidden long-tail terms.

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**

- Targeting head terms exclusively — long-tail clusters convert better and are more achievable.
- Ignoring SERP features — if Google shows a featured snippet, AI Overview, or local pack for a keyword, your click-through rate drops dramatically even at position 1.
- Not revisiting keyword strategy quarterly — search intent shifts, especially in AI-affected SERPs. [FAST-CHANGING]

### Lesson 3: Technical SEO at Scale

**Concept Explanation (~500 words)**

Technical SEO is the infrastructure layer — if it's broken, great content doesn't rank. At an advanced level, you're managing technical health across large sites (1,000+ pages) and integrating with engineering teams.

**Core Web Vitals (CWV)**
Google's page experience signals — LCP (Largest Contentful Paint), INP (Interaction to Next Paint, replaced FID in 2024), and CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift) — are ranking factors. For advanced practitioners, CWV optimization is a collaboration with front-end engineers.

Key levers:
- LCP: Preload hero images, use a CDN, optimize server response time (TTFB).
- INP: Reduce JavaScript main-thread blocking; defer non-critical scripts.
- CLS: Set explicit width/height on images and embeds; avoid dynamic content insertion above the fold.

Measure CWV in Google Search Console > Core Web Vitals report, and validate field data with CrUX (Chrome User Experience Report) via BigQuery for large-scale analysis.

**Crawl Efficiency**
For large sites, Googlebot has a crawl budget — it won't crawl every page. Wasted crawl budget = pages that never get indexed.

Improve crawl efficiency:
- Block low-value URLs (faceted navigation, search result pages, duplicate print versions) via robots.txt or noindex.
- Fix redirect chains — every hop costs crawl budget.
- Submit XML sitemaps and validate in Search Console.
- Use log file analysis (Screaming Frog Log Analyzer, Splunk) to see exactly which pages Googlebot is crawling and how often.

**Schema Markup**
Structured data (JSON-LD format preferred by Google) enables rich results — star ratings, FAQ dropdowns, How-To steps, breadcrumbs. These boost CTR significantly without changing rankings directly.

Implement schema for: Articles, FAQs, Products, Reviews, Events, BreadcrumbList, SiteLinks. Validate with Google's Rich Results Test.

**Site Architecture**
Flat architecture (maximum 3 clicks from homepage to any page) distributes PageRank efficiently. Use internal links strategically — every link from a high-authority page boosts the target page.

**Real-World Case Study**

When a major e-commerce retailer (commonly cited in technical SEO conference talks) discovered via log file analysis that Googlebot was spending 60% of its crawl budget on faceted navigation URLs (e.g., /shoes?color=red&size=10), adding a noindex+nofollow rule to those parameter URLs freed the budget for product and category pages. Organic traffic to core category pages increased within two crawl cycles. The lesson: crawl budget is finite; spend it on pages that matter.

**AI Tools for This Lesson**

- **Screaming Frog SEO Spider** — Comprehensive site crawler for technical audits.
- **Ahrefs Site Audit / Semrush Site Audit** — Cloud-based auditing with prioritized issue lists.
- **Google Search Console** — Free; the ground truth for indexing, crawl errors, and CWV field data.
- **ChatGPT / Claude** — Generate JSON-LD schema markup from a page description.
  - *Prompt:* "Generate valid JSON-LD schema markup for a FAQ page about [topic]. Include 5 questions and answers. Use the FAQPage schema type."
- **PageSpeed Insights / WebPageTest** — CWV measurement and waterfall analysis.

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**

- Implementing noindex on pages that have valuable backlinks — you lose link equity.
- Using meta refresh redirects instead of 301s — search engines treat these differently.
- Ignoring mobile-first indexing — Google indexes the mobile version of your site; desktop-only optimization is wasted effort.
- Adding schema to content that doesn't reflect what's on the page — Google may penalize for misleading structured data.

### Lesson 4: AI-Powered Content Production at Scale

**Concept Explanation (~500 words)**

AI-generated content is now standard practice. The question is no longer "should we use AI?" but "how do we use AI while maintaining quality signals that satisfy both users and Google's Helpful Content system?"

Google's guidance is clear: the *source* of content doesn't matter; the *quality and helpfulness* does. AI content that is shallow, generic, and lacks original perspective will be devalued. AI content that's edited by subject matter experts, enriched with original data, and genuinely helps users is treated the same as human-written content.

**The Advanced AI Content Workflow**

1. **Brief generation.** Use AI to generate a comprehensive content brief: target keyword, intent, suggested structure, entities to mention, competitor angles to differentiate from, word count estimate.

2. **First draft.** Use Claude, ChatGPT, or a specialized tool like Jasper/Copy.ai to generate a first draft from the brief. Do NOT publish this draft as-is.

3. **Expert enrichment.** Have a subject matter expert (or yourself) add:
   - Original perspective or opinion
   - First-hand experience or case studies
   - Data that isn't publicly available (proprietary research, client results)
   - Quotes from real people

4. **Entity optimization.** Use Clearscope or Surfer to check that the content covers all relevant entities and questions a comprehensive resource would include.

5. **Fact-checking.** AI hallucinates statistics, dates, and citations. Every factual claim needs verification before publication.

6. **Humanization layer.** Vary sentence structure, inject personality, and remove AI tells (excessive listicles, hollow transition phrases, overuse of "delve into").

7. **E-E-A-T signals.** Ensure the author bio is real and credible, the page has proper citations, and the content demonstrates genuine experience with the topic.

**Scaling content production:**
At agency or enterprise scale, build a content assembly line:
- AI generates 80% of the draft
- Junior editors fact-check and polish
- Senior editor/SME reviews for accuracy and adds original insight
- SEO specialist does final optimization pass

This approach can produce 10–20 high-quality articles per month per editor, versus 2–4 fully manual.

**Real-World Case Study**

The Washington Post's Heliograf system and AP's Automated Insights have used AI to generate thousands of sports scores, financial earnings reports, and local news articles since 2016. In marketing content, brands like Nestlé have used AI to generate product description variants at scale across thousands of SKUs. The lesson: AI excels at structured, templatable content; humans excel at narrative, opinion, and original research.

**AI Tools for This Lesson** [FAST-CHANGING — tool landscape shifts quarterly]

- **Claude (Anthropic)** — Best for long-form, nuanced content and instruction-following. Strong at maintaining tone consistency.
- **ChatGPT (OpenAI)** — Versatile; strong for brainstorming, outlines, and conversational content.
- **Jasper** — Marketing-specific; has workflow templates for blog posts, ads, emails.
- **Surfer SEO "Surfy" AI** — Writes and optimizes simultaneously with live content scores.
- **Clearscope** — Post-draft optimization; shows entity coverage score.
- **Originality.ai / GPTZero** — AI detection tools (useful for agencies managing writers). Note: detection accuracy is imperfect; use as quality signal, not proof.

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**

- Publishing unedited AI output — Google's systems and savvy readers will spot shallow content.
- Using AI for YMYL (Your Money or Your Life) topics without expert review — health, finance, legal content needs verified expert oversight.
- Not tracking AI content performance separately — you need to know if AI-assisted posts perform differently than manual posts.
- Over-relying on AI for link-earning content — original research and data studies outperform AI summaries for link acquisition.

### Lesson 5: Link Building & Digital PR Systems

**Concept Explanation (~500 words)**

Link building in 2026 is about earning links, not manipulating them. Google's spam systems (Penguin, SpamBrain) and manual review teams have become highly effective at identifying link schemes. The advanced approach: build systems that generate links as a *byproduct* of good marketing.

**Tier 1: Original Research & Data Studies**
Original research — surveys, proprietary data analysis, industry reports — is the highest-leverage link-building asset. Journalists, bloggers, and content creators need statistics and data. If you publish the primary source, you become the citation.

Process:
1. Survey your customer base or a third-party panel (Pollfish, SurveyMonkey Audience) on a relevant industry question.
2. Analyze and visualize the data (Google Sheets, Tableau, Datawrapper).
3. Write a press-release-style announcement and a full report.
4. Pitch to industry journalists and bloggers who cover your space.

A single well-executed study can earn dozens to hundreds of editorial backlinks from high-DR domains.

**Tier 2: Digital PR Campaigns**
Digital PR blends traditional PR storytelling with SEO link goals. Create campaign assets — data visualizations, interactive tools, compelling narratives — that journalists find inherently newsworthy or useful.

Examples of linkable assets:
- Interactive calculators (mortgage calculator, carbon footprint tool)
- Free downloadable templates (content calendar, budget spreadsheet)
- Industry cost index or salary reports
- "State of the industry" annual reports

**Tier 3: Broken Link Building & Resource Page Outreach**
Find pages in your niche with broken external links (Ahrefs: Site Explorer > Pages > Broken Backlinks on competitor sites). Offer your relevant content as a replacement. This provides genuine value to the site owner.

Similarly, find "resource" or "tools" pages that list helpful links in your industry and pitch inclusion.

**AI-Assisted Outreach**
Personalized outreach at scale used to require hours of manual research. AI can now:
- Research a prospect's recent content and identify a genuine connection to your asset
- Draft a personalized first-line for each email ("I noticed your piece on [X] cited [Y] — our new study updates that finding...")
- A/B test subject lines and body copy variations

**Real-World Case Study**

Backlinko (Brian Dean) built one of the most-linked-to marketing blogs in the world largely through original research. His "Google Ranking Factors" study, "We Analyzed X Million Backlinks" reports, and "SEO Industry" survey became the go-to citations for the SEO industry. Each study generated thousands of referring domains without any outreach required beyond initial promotion. The lesson: become the primary source, not the aggregator.

**AI Tools for This Lesson**

- **Ahrefs / Semrush** — Backlink analysis, competitor link research, broken link finding.
- **Hunter.io / Apollo.io** — Email finder for outreach prospecting.
- **Claude / ChatGPT** — Draft personalized outreach emails and PR pitches.
  - *Prompt:* "Write a personalized link-building outreach email to a journalist who recently wrote about [topic]. Reference their specific article. Our new study found [finding]. Keep it under 150 words and end with a clear CTA."
- **Pitchbox / BuzzStream** — Outreach CRM for managing link-building campaigns at scale. [FAST-CHANGING]
- **HARO alternatives (Qwoted, Featured.com, Connectively)** — Respond to journalist queries to earn high-DR press mentions. Note: HARO was discontinued in 2023; verify current alternatives.

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**

- Buying links — Google's penalties are severe and can take months to recover from.
- Sending generic outreach emails — response rates are under 1%; personalization moves the needle.
- Building links to your homepage only — build links to your content cluster pages to boost their rankings directly.
- Ignoring no-follow links — editorial no-follow links from major publications drive real traffic and brand awareness even without direct PageRank.

### Lesson 6: Content Distribution & Organic Amplification

**Concept Explanation (~450 words)**

Publishing great content is table stakes. Distribution determines whether that content actually reaches its audience and earns the signals (clicks, shares, links, dwell time) that tell Google it's valuable.

**Owned Channels**
- Email list: Send every new piece to your subscribers. High-intent audience, immediate traffic signal.
- Push notifications: Web push (OneSignal, PushOwl) delivers new content alerts to opted-in users.
- RSS/podcast repurposing: Convert blog content to audio for podcast distribution.

**Earned/Shared Channels**
- Social seeding: Share across all brand social channels. Identify power users or community members who might share.
- Community posting: Reddit, LinkedIn groups, Slack communities, Discord servers relevant to your audience. Post value, not spam — participate genuinely.
- Content syndication: Republish on Medium, LinkedIn Articles, or industry publications with canonical tags pointing to your original.

**Paid Amplification**
Even a small budget ($200–$500) to promote your best content via social ads can dramatically accelerate link acquisition and sharing. Target people who write about your topic (journalists, bloggers) on LinkedIn and Twitter/X.

**Content Refreshing**
One of the highest-ROI activities in SEO: refreshing existing content that ranks on pages 2–4. Update statistics, add new sections, improve formatting, and re-promote. Pages that lost traffic due to freshness often bounce back to page 1 after a thorough refresh with a new publish date.

**AI Tools for This Lesson**

- **Buffer / Hootsuite / Publer** — Schedule social distribution across channels. Some include AI caption generation. [FAST-CHANGING: AI features vary by plan]
- **Claude / ChatGPT** — Repurpose a long-form blog post into 10 LinkedIn posts, 5 Twitter/X threads, an email newsletter, and a video script.
  - *Prompt:* "Transform the following blog post into: (1) a 5-tweet thread, (2) a 500-word LinkedIn post, (3) a 200-word email newsletter intro. Blog post: [paste content]"
- **Quuu Promote / SparkToro** — Content promotion networks and audience research.
- **Exploding Topics / BuzzSumo** — Identify trending topics to time your content distribution for maximum social momentum.

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**

- Publishing and immediately moving on — most content needs 6+ distribution touchpoints to gain traction.
- Using identical copy across all channels — adapt format and tone for each platform.
- Never refreshing evergreen content — content decays; traffic drops without maintenance.

### Lesson 7: SEO Analytics & Revenue Attribution

**Concept Explanation (~500 words)**

At the advanced level, SEO reporting stops being about rankings and starts being about revenue. The question stakeholders care about: "What revenue did organic search generate this month, and is it growing?"

**GA4 Setup for SEO**
Google Analytics 4 replaced Universal Analytics in 2024. Key configurations for SEO:
- Set up conversion events for your primary actions (form submission, purchase, free trial signup, demo request).
- Create an "Organic Search" channel group that correctly attributes SEO traffic (verify it doesn't include brand paid search).
- Use the "Landing Page" dimension to see which SEO pages drive the most conversions.
- Enable Google Signals for cross-device tracking (requires user consent in GDPR regions).

**Search Console + GA4 Integration**
Connect Google Search Console to GA4 to see which queries lead to which landing pages and what conversion rate those sessions achieve. This reveals your highest-converting keywords — often different from your highest-volume keywords.

**Attribution Models**
Default last-click attribution undercounts SEO's contribution. Organic search often starts the buyer journey but gets no credit when a paid ad or direct visit converts.

Switch to **data-driven attribution** in GA4 (available when you have sufficient conversion volume). This uses machine learning to distribute credit across all touchpoints proportionally.

For agencies: build custom attribution dashboards in Looker Studio connecting GA4, Search Console, and CRM data (HubSpot, Salesforce).

**SEO ROI Calculation**
Basic formula:
```
SEO ROI = ((Revenue from Organic - Cost of SEO) / Cost of SEO) × 100
```

Cost of SEO includes: content production, tools, link building, technical work, and your time.

Revenue from Organic: pull from GA4 or CRM filtered by organic source.

For SaaS businesses, factor in LTV (Lifetime Value): 
```
Organic Revenue = (Organic Conversions × Conversion Rate to Paid × LTV)
```

**Rank Tracking vs. Business Metrics**
Rank tracking (Ahrefs, Semrush, SERPBot) is a leading indicator. Business metrics (traffic, conversions, revenue) are lagging indicators. Report both but focus conversations with stakeholders on business metrics.

**Real-World Case Study**

A B2B SaaS company (commonly cited in GA4 migration case studies) discovered after implementing data-driven attribution in GA4 that organic search was influencing 40% of their closed deals as a first-touch channel, even though last-click attribution gave it credit for only 8% of conversions. This finding tripled the SEO budget allocation. The lesson: attribution model choice dramatically changes how stakeholders value your channel.

**AI Tools for This Lesson**

- **Google Analytics 4** — Free; essential baseline.
- **Google Looker Studio** — Free; build custom dashboards combining multiple data sources.
- **Semrush / Ahrefs** — Rank tracking and Share of Voice measurement.
- **Claude / ChatGPT** — Write Looker Studio calculated metrics formulas, GA4 exploration queries, and executive summary narratives.
  - *Prompt:* "Write an executive summary for an SEO report. Organic traffic grew 23% MoM. Conversions from organic increased 31%. Top pages: [list]. Top keywords: [list]. Keep it to 3 bullet points and a recommended next action."

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**

- Reporting rankings to C-suite — they care about revenue, not position 4 vs. position 7.
- Not accounting for seasonality — compare YoY, not just MoM.
- Attributing all traffic growth to SEO during periods of brand activity (PR, campaigns) — isolate variables in your attribution.
- Ignoring Google Search Console impressions — they show whether your visibility is improving even before traffic catches up.

## Practical Tasks

### Task 1: Full-Site Topical Authority Audit & Cluster Map

**Objective:** Audit an existing website (your own, a client's, or a provided test site) and produce a complete topical authority cluster map with prioritized content gaps.

**Deliverable:** A Google Sheets cluster map + a written strategic brief (800–1,000 words).

**Step-by-Step Workflow**

*AI-Assisted Version:*
1. Export all URLs from the site using Screaming Frog (free up to 500 URLs).
2. Paste the URL list into Claude with the prompt: "Analyze these URLs and group them into content clusters by topic. Identify the likely pillar pages. Flag any sub-topics that appear underserved (fewer than 2 cluster pages)."
3. Cross-reference with Ahrefs or Semrush to pull traffic and keyword data for each URL.
4. Use Semrush's Keyword Gap tool to find competitor topics not covered on the site.
5. Build a cluster map in Google Sheets: Tab 1 = existing clusters; Tab 2 = gap opportunities; Tab 3 = priority queue (scored by opportunity × difficulty).
6. Write the strategic brief explaining your findings, top 3 recommendations, and 90-day content roadmap.

*Manual Version:*
1. Manually review the site's navigation, blog categories, and top pages in GA4.
2. Create the cluster map by hand, grouping pages by topic.
3. Research 3 competitors manually using their sitemaps and Semrush's free tier.
4. Identify 10 content gaps and prioritize by estimated search volume.
5. Write the strategic brief.

**Grading Rubric**

| Criterion | Points |
|-----------|--------|
| Cluster map completeness (all major topics identified, accurately grouped) | 25 |
| Gap analysis quality (gaps are real, search-validated, and prioritized) | 20 |
| Strategic brief clarity and actionability | 20 |
| Data accuracy (keyword volumes, difficulty scores cited correctly) | 15 |
| Presentation quality (clean, readable, LMS-ready format) | 10 |
| 90-day roadmap is realistic and sequenced correctly | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

### Task 2: Technical SEO Audit Report

**Objective:** Conduct a full technical SEO audit of a website and produce a prioritized remediation plan.

**Deliverable:** A formatted audit report with categorized issues and a fix-priority matrix.

**Step-by-Step Workflow**

*AI-Assisted Version:*
1. Run Screaming Frog on the target site; export all data as CSV.
2. Run Google PageSpeed Insights on the top 5 most-trafficked pages.
3. Export Google Search Console Coverage and Core Web Vitals reports.
4. Paste the crawl summary data into Claude: "You are an SEO consultant. Analyze this crawl data and identify the top 10 technical issues by SEO impact. For each issue: describe the problem, explain why it matters, and give step-by-step fix instructions. Prioritize by impact × ease of fix."
5. Validate structured data using Google's Rich Results Test on 5 pages.
6. Compile findings into a prioritized report: P0 (critical), P1 (high), P2 (medium), P3 (low).

*Manual Version:*
1. Complete steps 1–3 above.
2. Manually analyze the crawl data for: broken links, redirect chains, missing meta tags, duplicate content, thin pages, missing alt text, slow pages.
3. Research each issue's SEO impact independently.
4. Build the report manually.

**Grading Rubric**

| Criterion | Points |
|-----------|--------|
| Issue identification completeness (all major issue types checked) | 25 |
| Prioritization accuracy (P0 issues are genuinely critical) | 20 |
| Fix instructions are actionable and technically accurate | 25 |
| Core Web Vitals analysis is correct and improvement actions are realistic | 15 |
| Report is client-ready: clear language, no unexplained jargon | 15 |
| **Total** | **100** |

## Assessment

### Multiple-Choice Quiz (10 Questions)

**Q1.** Which of the following best describes "topical authority" in modern SEO?

- A) Having the highest Domain Rating on Ahrefs
- B) Owning comprehensive, interlinked coverage of an entire subject area
- C) Targeting the most keywords on a single page
- D) Publishing content daily regardless of topic relevance

**Correct Answer: B**
*Explanation: Topical authority is built by comprehensively covering a subject through a cluster of interlinked content, signaling to Google that your site is the definitive resource on that topic. Domain Rating is a proxy for link authority, not topical relevance.*

---

**Q2.** A page targeting "best project management software" generates 10,000 monthly impressions in Search Console but has a 2% CTR. Which action is MOST likely to improve CTR without changing rankings?

- A) Add more keywords to the page body
- B) Build more backlinks to the page

### Short-Answer Questions

**Q1.** Explain the difference between Domain Authority (DA) and Topical Authority, and describe how an advanced SEO practitioner would prioritize one over the other when building a content strategy for a new niche site.

**Model Answer:**
Domain Authority (or Domain Rating in Ahrefs) is a third-party metric estimating the overall link-based strength of a domain — essentially a proxy for how many high-quality sites link to you. It is a site-wide signal.

Topical Authority is about the depth and comprehensiveness of coverage within a specific subject area. A site can have low DA but high topical authority in a niche (for example, a specialized B2B software review site with fewer total links but extensive coverage of its category).

For a new niche site, an advanced practitioner would *prioritize topical authority first*. Building DA takes time and significant link acquisition. But by publishing comprehensive, interlinked content clusters on a narrow topic set, a new site can rank for long-tail and mid-tail terms within that niche relatively quickly — even without many backlinks — because Google's systems recognize it as a relevant, authoritative resource within that subject space. DA-building (through link acquisition) runs in parallel but is a longer-term effort.

---

**Q2.** A client asks why their organic traffic has dropped 30% over the past three months despite maintaining their rankings. What are the three most likely causes you would investigate first, and what data would you use to diagnose each one?

**Model Answer:**

1. **SERP feature cannibalization.** Google may have added AI Overviews, Featured Snippets, or other SERP features for the client's top keywords, reducing organic click-through rates even without ranking changes. *Diagnose with:* Google Search Console — compare impressions vs. clicks over time. If impressions are stable but CTR has dropped, SERP features are likely the cause.

2. **Algorithm update impact.** Google releases core updates that can demote entire categories of content (thin content, low E-E-A-T pages). *Diagnose with:* Check Google's confirmed update dates (Google Search Central blog) against the traffic drop date. Use Google Search Console's Performance report segmented by query type to see if specific page categories lost visibility.

3. **Seasonality or external demand shift.** Demand for the client's keywords may have naturally declined seasonally or due to a market trend shift. *Diagnose with:* Google Trends for the core keyword set. Compare YoY traffic in GA4 (not just MoM) to control for seasonal patterns. Check if industry-wide search volume has changed using Ahrefs or Semrush historical data.

---

**Q3.** You are pitching an SEO program to a CMO who has previously invested in paid search only. How would you frame the ROI of SEO compared to paid search, and what would you need to prove your case?

**Model Answer:**
The core ROI argument for SEO vs. paid search comes down to compounding return vs. linear return.

Paid search delivers traffic while the budget runs — stop paying, traffic stops. SEO investment compounds: content and links built today generate traffic for months or years. The cost-per-click equivalent of organic traffic improves over time as more keywords rank.

To prove the case, I would:

1. **Pull the client's Google Ads spend and CPC data.** Calculate what they're currently paying per click for their top keywords in paid search.
2. **Use Ahrefs or Semrush to estimate organic traffic value** — the platform calculates what that traffic would cost if purchased via Google Ads. Show the "organic traffic value" metric as a cost-savings comparison.
3. **Project a 12-month SEO roadmap** with expected traffic milestones (conservative, base, and optimistic scenarios) and the cost of achieving them vs. buying equivalent paid traffic.
4. **Show a competitor organic case study** to demonstrate that a peer in their industry is successfully driving significant traffic through SEO.
5. **Propose a hybrid approach** — run paid on high-intent terms while SEO builds, then scale back paid as organic rankings develop. This reduces risk and shows I'm not replacing their current investment, just complementing it.
